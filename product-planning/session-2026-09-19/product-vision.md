# Product vision

Version 0.8 — reflects founder priorities from 2026-09-20; exact wording remains proposed.

## Vision

People can turn a shared desire to do something together into a decision and a real meetup quickly, without getting stuck in endless group discussion.

The intended audience is any group arranging a meetup, including friends, families, colleagues, and communities. Launch in Tashkent, with Uzbekistan as the broader intended market.

## Mission

Help groups choose a date and place that work for them, reach a decision, and remember to meet. Support a chosen destination or AI-assisted discovery.

## Product promise

“Find a date that works, choose a place that fits, and remember the meetup.”

The founder named all three as core value, then described reminders as tentative. The desired outcome of remembering meetups remains recorded; reminders are not a committed initial feature. Their scope remains open. Planning still begins with a person initiating.

This promise depends on proving that maintaining availability and preferences does not create excessive work.

## Proposed principles

1. Optimize for completed, worthwhile meetups and reduced effort.
2. Treat willingness as explicit; unoccupied calendar time is not permission.
3. Keep sensitive constraints private, including in explanations and notifications.
4. Ask for attention only when there is a useful action to take.
5. Make participation, confirmation, and cancellation rules understandable.
6. Watch for repeated exclusion; let groups decide acceptable participation rules.
7. Make AI useful through better time and place suggestions. Use reliable structured inputs and matching where appropriate; AI assistance does not imply authority to commit members.
8. Begin planning when a person initiates. Reduce the work that follows using the group's preferences, recent meetups, and timetables.
9. Respect decisions the group has already made. Offer a known-place workflow and an assisted discovery workflow; the latter uses three questions and internet search.

## Longer-term possibility

The founder explicitly wants a large product that helps people meet and make decisions faster. The initial problem is holiday meetup planning in Uzbekistan. Broader group decision-making is a possible interpretation of this ambition, not yet an agreed expansion scope. A narrow first use case should establish the behavior on which the larger product can grow.

The thesis proposes temporary human availability as a broader foundation for friends, experts, creators, and professional capacity. These audiences have different needs and business models. Expansion should follow evidence of a reusable behavior, rather than determine the first release.

## Still to establish with the founder

Pilot group composition; personal motivation beyond the stated ambition; which decisions the long-term product should support; boundaries on automated decisions; and what success should look like over the first year.
